After Pat Gorman aligned the Miata, the front end still felt light at speed. The hood vents helped a little, but I wanted to try more substantial aero. During a Fourth of July sale, I ordered the RGR Engineering Tipo 1 kit with side skirts compatible with the NB Tupperware setup. The kit cost about $1,700 shipped to my home.

## Fitting the Front Splitter

The basic mounting layout was straightforward: a splitter support brace, brackets, and mounting points near the factory sway bar mounts. My car has an AWR heavy-duty sway bar mount, though, and its front end is slightly uneven from an earlier accident. That made the fit less straightforward than the instructions suggested.

The supplied M6 × 1.0 × 25 mm bolts didn't give me the spacing I needed with the AWR bracket. I made PETG spacers measuring roughly 15 mm on the driver side and 20 mm on the passenger side to mock up the fit. The difference reflected the car's uneven front end. I also installed my JetStream front lip, which meant drilling the bumper.

After fitting the lip and splitter, I noticed a gap near the front of the tire. I used eye hooks, 3/32-inch steel cable, cable clamps, and nuts to support the edge while I worked on the fitment. I want to revisit that mounting arrangement before relying on it for track use.

## Rear Diffuser

The rear diffuser was much easier. I fitted the supplied adapters in the original rear mounting holes and attached the diffuser to the support brackets. The rear support bolts were frustrating to reinstall; they may have been cross-threaded previously, but I need to inspect them properly before calling the job finished.

## Still to Sort Out

The front splitter fitment and support hardware need another pass. I originally intended to film the installation, so I don't have the photos I want for a detailed walkthrough yet. I'll update this post once I've taken the aero back off, checked the mounting points, and documented the final setup.
