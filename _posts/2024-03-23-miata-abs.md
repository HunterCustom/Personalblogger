My 1999 Mazda Miata is a California-spec, non-ABS car. I'm researching an ABS conversion using a system newer than the original Miata unit.

Why not use the factory ABS? Well, the factory ABS, while functional, is often regarded as quite basic. Many reviews suggest it's just the bare minimum. Let's face it, which ABS system from the early 2000s was truly decent?

The system I'm researching is the BMW MK60 ABS. This system is well-known in grassroots motorsports circles for being relatively inexpensive compared to the Bosch Motorsports ABS system, which costs around $10,000 (that's more than I paid for the Miata in 2021).

## MK60 Parts and Research

I started with [TrackTuned's MK60 swap guide](https://www.tracktuned.com/feed/mk60absswapguide), which documents an installation in a 350Z. Its parts list is a useful starting point, though I still need to verify what applies to my Miata.

Parts needed:

- MK60 ABS unit. Candidate ATE part numbers: 10.0960-813.3 (E46 M3), 10.0960-818.3 (E46 M3), and 10.0960-817.3 (E46 M3 CSL).
I originally looked at 3DM Motorsports' standalone harness, but it was no longer listed when I wrote these notes. I plan to build my own harness. A [rusefi forum discussion](https://rusefi.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=2454) suggests that firmware options vary by controller version. I still need to verify the exact unit and calibration before buying anything.

-  MK60 Yaw Sensor
- Two MK60 pressure sensors
- MK60 wiring connectors, ideally with at least 12 inches of harness lead
- Wiring supplies, fuses, and any required resistors (specifications to verify)
- Brake-line fittings and adapters (sizes to verify)

My current shortlist is the MK60 unit, yaw sensor, two pressure sensors, and wiring connectors. I'm especially interested in an 813.3 controller, but I need to research its calibration options further. Repinning the connectors may be cleaner than splicing, depending on cost and availability.

## Parts for the Miata

Since my Miata doesn't have factory ABS rings, I'll need to either acquire OEM ABS rings from a junkyard NB or opt for new hubs. I've decided to go with the latter and purchase new hubs from [MiataHubs](https://www.miatahubs.com). Although these hubs are highly priced, I believe they are worth the investment, as OEM Miata hubs are known to break under the pressures of track use, even with stock power (which is what I have at the time of writing this). Now onto the next task...

### Wheel Speed Sensors

Getting straight to the point, I'm contemplating installing OEM ABS knuckles for my car. I already have plans to coat the entire suspension system in POR-15, while also upgrading bushings with Supermiata's bushing set. (I acquired these when they were originally released in 2020 or 2021 and haven't yet installed them.) Since I'll already have my entire suspension disassembled, what's the difficulty level of swapping the knuckles on my Miata?

I still need to confirm which Miata wheel-speed sensors are compatible with the MK60. I don't want to infer sensor type from connector pin count alone.

While I was looking into this I found a helpful post on the [MiataTurbo forums](https://www.miataturbo.net/haltech-118/%5Bhow-%5D-vss-abs-sensor-na-nb-107705/) for using the factory miata ABS sensors as a wheel speed sensors for the haltech ECU (I'm using a Haltech Elite 1500 ECU)

I found used OEM knuckles on Cap-Parts.com priced at about $175 to $200, but they were located well over 200 miles away. This price seemed steep, and I doubted that most lower-costing places would ship the knuckles to me. Additionally, I would need to coat them in POR-15. However, I managed to find a whole brand new set from Mazda for $700, covering all four corners, in brand new pristine condition. This means I wouldn't have to coat them, and I can be assured of their excellent quality. This looks like the path I'll be taking.

### Update: October 23, 2024

I put the ABS plan on the back burner while I worked through the budget and the rest of the build. With the engine out, this could be a good opportunity to run new brake lines, if the budget allows. 

I was able to obtain OEM ABS axles with the tone rings for about $200 from [Expoline Auto](https://expolineauto.com). They were happy to see the Miata again and curious about my plans for it. 
I'm no longer planning to use POR-15 because of the cost. Someone suggested [Majic Tractor Enamel Paint](https://www.tractorsupply.com/tsc/product/majic-tractor-truck-implement-oil-base-enamel-matte-black-1-gal) as a cheaper alternative: at the prices I saw, POR-15 was $220 per gallon and Majic was $60 per gallon.

### ABS Pump Label Notes

The pump uses German abbreviations. My working notes are: **VR** = front right, **VL** = front left, **HR** = rear right, and **HL** = rear left. I have **H** marked as a rear input, but I need to confirm every port against documentation before plumbing the brakes.

I may be able to retain the OEM brake master cylinder. The master cylinder, proportioning valve, pressure sensor, and ABS pump plumbing still need to be verified before installation.
